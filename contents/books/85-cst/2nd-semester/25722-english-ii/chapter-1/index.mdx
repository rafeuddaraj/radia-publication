# People or Institutions Making History

## NELSON MANDELA, FROM APARTHEID FIGHTER TO PRESIDENT

### 1.1. Talk about the world-famous personality

Nelson Mandela, born on July 18, 1918, in the village of Mvezo, South Africa, was one of the most iconic leaders in the 20th century. He became internationally recognized for his steadfast opposition to apartheid—a racially discriminatory system enforced by the South African government. Mandela’s unwavering commitment to justice, equality, and human rights led him to become the first Black president of South Africa in 1994, marking the end of institutionalized racism in the country. His life story—from being a freedom fighter and prisoner to a global symbol of peace and reconciliation—is a testament to his resilience and dedication to fighting for what is right.

### 1.2. Know some renowned speeches of Nelson Mandela

- **"I Am Prepared to Die" (1964)**: Delivered during his trial, this speech emphasized Mandela's commitment to fighting apartheid, even at the cost of his own life. It is widely regarded as one of the most powerful statements against the injustice of the apartheid regime.
  
- **"An Ideal for Which I Am Prepared to Die" (1990)**: After his release from prison, Mandela gave a speech addressing the importance of continued struggle for equality and justice, symbolizing hope for a new South Africa.

- **"We Are the Builders of the Future" (1993)**: A speech he delivered during the Nobel Peace Prize acceptance, calling for reconciliation, peace, and a new democratic South Africa, focusing on uniting the country rather than dividing it.

### 1.3. Understand the meaning of confusing words

- **Apartheid**: A system of institutionalized racial segregation and discrimination in South Africa, enforced by the government from 1948 to the early 1990s.
- **Reconciliation**: The process of restoring friendly relations between groups or individuals after conflict, often involving forgiveness and understanding.
- **Mandela’s “Ubuntu” Philosophy**: Ubuntu is a Southern African term meaning "I am because we are," emphasizing interconnectedness, mutual respect, and collective humanity.
  
### 1.4. Develop reading, speaking & listening skills

To develop your skills, follow these steps:

1. **Reading**: Read more about Nelson Mandela’s speeches, interviews, and writings to gain deeper insights into his life and philosophies.
  
2. **Speaking**: Practice summarizing Mandela's speeches and expressing your thoughts about his ideas. Try to explain his concepts of justice and equality in your own words.

3. **Listening**: Watch videos or listen to audio recordings of Mandela’s speeches to get a better understanding of his tone, delivery, and powerful use of language.
